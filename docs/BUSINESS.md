# Mikanra business assessment — October 20, 2026

## Judgment

Mikanra is a plausible focused indie learning-game business. The current chapter demonstrates a product experience; willingness to pay, retention, learning transfer and acquisition cost remain unvalidated. No verified customer, revenue or retention evidence was supplied for this assessment.

Start with English-speaking adult beginners who like cozy games and want useful Japanese for everyday situations. Their job is to make regular practice appealing and meaningful. The cafe loop supplies a reason to understand an order, while recurring people give the player a reason to return. Travel preparation is a secondary use case; this is not job training or a complete Japanese course.

## Competition and differentiation

| Product | Relevant overlap | Mikanra's proposed focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Noun Town](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2313720/Noun_Town_Language_Learning/) | Contextual language play, cafe activities, recurring NPCs and vocabulary practice | A focused Japanese cafe story with physical recipes, relationships and gradually reduced support, accessible in a browser |
| [Influent](https://store.steampowered.com/app/274980/Influent/) | 3D vocabulary exploration and pronunciation | Connected social situations and running an establishment across days |
| [Lingo Legend](https://www.lingolegend.com/home) | Games designed to sustain language practice | Japanese comprehension directly serves the narrative and cafe tasks |

These competitors establish that language-learning games are an existing category; they do not prove demand for Mikanra. Avoid first-ever or category-invention claims. The defensible work is quality of authored situations, character attachment, curriculum curation, demonstrated learning transfer and a community that returns for new stories.

## Revenue options

| Model | Who pays / what they buy | Timing and constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free demo → paid story packs | Players buy new stories, locations, relationships and useful situations | Recommended first test. Proposed $14.99 expanded pack is a hypothesis, not an established price or current offer. |
| Contextual placement | Tea/coffee, stationery or cultural partners license approved placement in the world | Secondary revenue once qualified audience and engagement can be shown; no sponsor is currently committed. |
| Commissioned chapter | A cultural organization or brand funds an authored experience with agreed learning and presentation goals | Potential early service revenue; quote production, review, licensing, maintenance and reporting separately. |
| Subscription | Players fund dependable ongoing lessons and stories | Defer until a sustainable release cadence and sufficient content justify recurring payment. |
| Institutional license | Teachers or schools buy classroom use and support | Later: needs curriculum fit, instructor review, learning evidence, administration and privacy work. |

Sponsors buy either meaningful reach or a commissioned experience. A generic logo placement with no audience evidence is a weak starting offer. Paid story packs give the consumer business an independent revenue path. Optional partner experiences should fit the setting, respect editorial control and never sell learning progress or require purchases to understand an order.

## Economics: assumptions, not a forecast

The game currently runs in the browser without per-turn LLM inference or a developer-operated backend. Costs still include hosting/bandwidth, content writing, Japanese-language review, audio/art work, testing, support, fees, refunds and customer acquisition. Founder time is a real cost.

At a hypothetical $14.99 price, 100 buyers produce $1,499 gross and 1,000 produce $14,990 gross, before those costs. These are arithmetic scenarios, not expected sales. A hypothetical $1,500 content budget requires at least 101 purchases even before fees and acquisition, so a low sticker price alone is not evidence of profitability.

Use: contribution per buyer = actual net receipts minus variable service/support cost minus acquisition cost. Break-even buyers = fixed chapter cost / positive contribution per buyer, rounded up. Collect actual channel and cost data before projecting profit. Separate cash expenditure from uncompensated founder hours and recurring consumer revenue from one-off commissioned work.

Acquisition hypotheses: short gameplay clips, cozy-game creators, beginner Japanese communities, and Japan-interest communities. Use approved community channels and measure the path from qualified visitor to first shift, return visit and paid interest. No outreach or paid acquisition has been performed as part of this release.

## A directional 30-day validation pilot

Recruit 20 consenting adult beginners through the founder's own channels. Record prior Japanese experience; observe the first shift without coaching; ask what motivated or blocked them. Arrange a return session within a week and test familiar phrases in unfamiliar order combinations after a delay. Get a qualified Japanese instructor to review the content and synthetic pronunciation.

Track first-shift completion, time/help needed, voluntary return, delayed unassisted comprehension, desire for the next chapter and purchase commitments at the stated $14.99 concept price. Distinguish stated interest from an actual paid commitment. Use a manually run research log initially; the current game has no telemetry.

Pilot decision targets, selected for this project rather than industry benchmarks: at least 12/20 complete the first shift without founder intervention; at least 8/20 voluntarily return within seven days; identify and fix comprehension failures before expansion. Interview participants about the proposed paid pack and only build it when the founder has concrete demand evidence and an affordable production plan. This sample is exploratory and does not prove causal educational efficacy.

Next gates: content/instructor review, a repeat-play pilot, paid demand evidence, then a limited partner pilot. Mobile play, a full curriculum, a subscription and school dashboards are expansion investments rather than release prerequisites.
