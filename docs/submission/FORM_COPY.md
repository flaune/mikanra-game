# Submission form copy

## Project name

Mikanra

## One-line description

A cozy browser game where Japanese beginners learn through running a neighborhood cafe and building relationships with its regulars.

## Tell us about your business

Mikanra helps Japanese beginners turn short practice sessions into meaningful everyday experiences. Players take a job at Cafe Mikan, understand customers' orders, prepare drinks and grow closer to the people who return each morning. Japanese is useful because it helps the player participate in the neighborhood.

The current playable chapter spans seven mornings, progressing from a supported first shift to a neighborhood reading evening. It combines physical coffee and tea preparation, service and checkout with greetings, quantities, prices, bundled pronunciation, notebook review and optional comprehension checks. English meanings and romaji gradually fade with learning evidence, while help remains available. Progress and relationships persist in the browser; the cafe remains playable after the story concludes.

The initial audience is adult Japanese beginners who enjoy cozy games. Our proposed business model is a free introductory experience followed by paid story packs with new characters, places and practical situations. A $14.99 expanded pack is a pricing hypothesis to validate. Contextual product placement and commissioned cultural or branded chapters could provide additional revenue. No payment system, sponsor agreement, validated conversion rate or revenue claim is represented by this demo.

The immediate next steps are Japanese-instructor review, an observed beginner playtest, delayed comprehension checks and evidence of repeat play and willingness to pay. Mikanra is a beginner chapter rather than a complete course or a proven fluency intervention.

Development disclosure: I conceived and directed Mikanra. Bolt.new created the initial playable game foundation. Substantial subsequent core development and testing were completed using OpenAI Codex. Rive CLI authored animated cafe preparation feedback; Three.js renders the procedural scene and Rapier handles collision. React powers the interface. OpenJTalk/Mei and FFmpeg produced the bundled synthetic pronunciation; a local script generated original arrival audio. Google Fonts provides typography. Full third-party notices and source are available in the repository. The current release replaces the earlier noncommercial train recording.
